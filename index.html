Preface

A single study rarely settles a question. Effects vary from sample to sample, from one operationalization to the next, and from one setting to another, and the studies that reach print are not a random draw from the studies that were run. Meta-analysis is the set of statistical tools for learning from a body of studies as a whole: estimating an average effect, describing how much true effects vary, explaining that variation, and judging how far the published record can be trusted. A systematic review is the disciplined process that decides which studies enter that analysis and how their results are extracted and appraised.

This book covers both. It starts from the logic of research synthesis and the systematic review workflow (question, protocol, search, screening, extraction, risk of bias), builds up the core statistical machinery (effect sizes, common-effect and random-effects models, heterogeneity, meta-regression, dependent effect sizes, publication bias, sensitivity analysis), and then moves to methods that have become standard in the last decade: robust variance estimation with correlated-and-hierarchical effects working models, selection models and robust Bayesian meta-analysis, Bayesian hierarchical models with informative heterogeneity priors, network meta-analysis, meta-analytic structural equation modeling, multivariate and dose-response models, individual participant data, diagnostic test accuracy, the synthesis of causal estimates from quasi-experiments, and certainty-of-evidence ratings with GRADE.

Who this book is for

The book is written for graduate students and researchers in psychology, medicine and public health, economics, management, and marketing who need to run a meta-analysis, review one, or read one critically. It assumes familiarity with regression and basic statistical inference, and some experience with R. Mathematical notation is used where it clarifies what an estimator does, and every method is paired with code that runs on publicly available data.

How the book is organized

Part I, Foundations, motivates research synthesis (Chapter 1), walks through the systematic review workflow and PRISMA 2020 (Chapter 2), and shows how to compute and convert effect sizes (Chapter 3).

Part II, Core Methods, develops common-effect and random-effects models (Chapter 4), heterogeneity, subgroups, and meta-regression (Chapter 5), dependent effect sizes (Chapter 6), publication bias (Chapter 7), and sensitivity analysis and diagnostics (Chapter 8).

Part III, Advanced Methods, covers Bayesian meta-analysis (Chapter 9), network meta-analysis (Chapter 10), meta-analytic structural equation modeling (Chapter 11), multivariate, dose-response, and individual participant data meta-analysis (Chapter 12), diagnostic test accuracy (Chapter 13), and the meta-analysis of causal estimates (Chapter 14).

Part IV, Reporting, turns to certainty of evidence and reporting standards (Chapter 15) and closes with a workflow checklist (Chapter 16).

The chapters can be read in order or used as a reference. Each one ends with key takeaways and pointers to further reading.

Software and reproducibility

All analyses use R. The workhorse is metafor (Viechtbauer 2026), with meta (Schwarzer 2026) for alternative interfaces, clubSandwich (Pustejovsky 2026) and robumeta (Fisher et al. 2023) for robust variance estimation, netmeta (Rücker et al. 2026), multinma (Phillippo 2026), and gemtc (van Valkenhoef and Kuiper 2026) for network meta-analysis, metaSEM (Cheung 2026) for structural equation models, bayesmeta (Roever 2025), brms (Bürkner 2025), and RoBMA (Bartoš and Maier 2025) for Bayesian models, and mada (Doebler 2025) for diagnostic accuracy. Example data come from metadat (Viechtbauer et al. 2026) and the datasets bundled with these packages, so every chunk can be rerun as printed.

The book is rebuilt from source on every change, and every code chunk is executed during that build; output shown in the text is the output the code produced. To reproduce the environment, install the packages listed in the DESCRIPTION file of the source repository. The Bayesian chapters need a working C++ toolchain for Stan, and the RoBMA and gemtc examples need JAGS.

# Core packages used throughout the book
install.packages(c(
  "metafor", "metadat", "meta", "clubSandwich", "robumeta", "netmeta",
  "metaSEM", "bayesmeta", "brms", "RoBMA", "multinma", "mada", "esc"
))
